# Code of conduct

*Short, because it has to be read. It applies to issues, pull requests, debates, term cards, and the record itself.*

## The conduct this project asks for

**Argue about claims, not about people's standing.** A contributor's familiarity with a tradition is not the topic. What they asserted, and what they cited, is.

**Name your sources.** No claim without one. For cards this is enforced by the build, and for debates by validation, so it is not a matter of manners here: it is the format.

**Do not use a recorded loss as a weapon.** This one is specific to what this project holds. A loss is a fact about *translation*, not a fault in a tradition. *"The Latin pax imports imperial imposition"* is a citable claim about a rendering; quoted as an accusation against Christianity it is a misuse of the record. Every tradition in this base has lost something in translation. That is the argument for recording losses, not against any of them.

**Do not proselytise at contributors.** Nobody here is a conversion target, and a commons that reads as a recruiting ground will lose the people whose knowledge it needs.

**Do not present a tradition's internal disagreement as its error.** Traditions argue with themselves, at length, and often better than outsiders do. Say which strand you are quoting.

**Do not mock a tradition, a language, or a script.** Including by treating a rendering as self-evidently absurd.

## Not welcome here

Harassment. Slurs. Doxxing, including of contributors who use a pseudonym.

Publishing anyone's personal information, or any material covered by the boundary rule in [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md): **restorative justice case data, and statements made by harmed people, never appear in this project.**

Editing another person's words. The contribution protocol detects this mechanically, and detection is not the same as permission having been given.

## Enforcement

A maintainer who sees a breach will, in order:

1. say so in the thread where it happened, plainly and without a lecture;
2. remove the contribution that breaches this, stating why;
3. for repetition, decline further contributions from that account.

Removals are recorded in public with a reason, as removals of debates are. Anyone affected can respond in the same thread, and the maintainer who acted does not decide the appeal about their own action — see [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md), which also records honestly that there is currently one maintainer, and that appeals are therefore weak.

## Reporting

Open an issue, or write to the maintainer listed in the repository profile if the report involves someone who can read the issues.

If you are not sure whether something is a breach, asking is not a burden. Asking is cheaper than the alternative, which is a commons quietly losing a contributor who did not want to make a fuss.
